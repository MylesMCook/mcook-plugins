## Josef Müller-Brockmann — *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*

[Publisher record](https://niggli.ch/products/rastersysteme-fur-die-visuelle-gestaltung)

A grid can organize type, images, margins, and repeated page elements into a coherent system. Treat column width, leading, and alignment as decisions about legibility and continuity, not decoration. Use the grid to support the reading task; it is not a requirement that every element occupy an identical cell.

## Timothy Samara — *Making and Breaking the Grid*

[Publisher record for the third edition](https://www.quarto.com/books/9780760381939/Making-and-Breaking-the-Grid-Third-Edition). This link does not establish the edition used in the original notes.

Start with an ordering structure, then depart from it deliberately when emphasis or meaning calls for tension. A broken grid is useful only if the viewer can still follow the content. Compare manuscript, column, modular, and hierarchical structures against the material at hand.

## Josef Albers — *Interaction of Color*

[Publisher record](https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300146936/interaction-of-color/)

Color is perceived in context. Evaluate a palette at its actual size, adjacency, background, and viewing conditions; swatches alone cannot settle the decision. Test contrast and emphasis in the composed artifact.

## Armin Hofmann — *Graphic Design Manual*

[Publisher record](https://niggli.ch/en/products/methodik-der-form-und-bildgestaltung)

Dot, line, interval, figure and ground, and tonal contrast offer a vocabulary for visual structure. Use their relationships to create rhythm and focus before adding ornament. The imported notes described image-based exercises; they did not retain page references.

## Cath Caldwell and Yolanda Zappaterra — *Editorial Design: Digital and Print*

[Publisher record](https://www.laurenceking.com/products/editorial-design)

Editorial design arranges text, image, hierarchy, and navigation around a story. Use a consistent structure that can adapt to screen size and reading mode. Credit both authors when naming the book; the imported notes mentioned Caldwell alone.

## Synthesis boundary

For product UI, dashboards, editorial work, branding, and print, prefer readable order, contextual judgment, and a coherent system with purposeful exceptions. Check accessibility and task success before treating a visual preference as a rule.
