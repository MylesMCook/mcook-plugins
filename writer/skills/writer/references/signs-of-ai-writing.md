# Cleanup checklist

Use this checklist when editing prose for clarity or a natural voice. It does
not establish who wrote a passage or whether a model was involved.

1. **Find the claim.** State what happened, what the reader should do, or what
   decision is needed. Delete a sentence when it adds only praise or ceremony.
2. **Check the evidence.** Keep names, numbers, sources, and limitations. Flag
   unsupported claims instead of supplying plausible details.
3. **Name the action.** Prefer a person or system doing something the reader can
   recognize. Explain an abstract benefit through its actual mechanism.
4. **Order for the reader.** Put the request or result before background when
   that helps the reader act. Keep each paragraph focused on one purpose.
5. **Trim repetition.** Remove repeated introductions, duplicated conclusions,
   and sentences that restate the heading without adding information.
6. **Match the speaker.** Keep the requested formality and vocabulary. Preserve
   first-person wording that is supplied; never invent experience or emotion.
7. **Read the revision as a whole.** Smooth awkward rhythm without mechanically
   changing every sentence or banning ordinary words and punctuation.

For example, when the source establishes the change:

> The update offers a remarkable advancement in account management.

can become:

> The update lets account owners download their invoices.

If the source does not establish invoice downloads, ask what changed or leave a
placeholder. Specificity must come from evidence.

than a quick pass. Return the edited text in the user's requested format.
